Is there a difference between Pepsi and Coca-Cola?

By Rachel Segal

When thirsty, nothing tastes better than an ice-cold Pepsi or Coca-Cola – or so we’re told. Both carbonated soft drinks were created in the late 19th century (Coca-Cola in 1886, followed by Pepsi in 1898), meaning they’ve been around longer than we – and our parents – have. Despite more than a century of somewhat similar branding and marketing successes and missteps (clear Crystal Pepsi or Coca-Cola’s Tab Clear, anyone?) and a more or less comparable list of main ingredients blind taste tests show that consumers can tell the two apart. So, are these American beverage behemoths more alike or different?
Below, three points make the case that the colas really do differ, followed by three that say they are basically the same.

There are distinct differences

In flavor and popularity

Pepsi’s 1975 Pepsi Challenge showcased its sweeter taste and lifted sales past Coca-Cola, whose sweeter New Coke flopped before Coca-Cola Classic and Diet Coke succeeded. PepsiCo outranked its rival on Forbes Leading Global Brands in 2024, but Coca-Cola leads US carbonated soft drinks.

In long-term branding and growth

With soda drinking falling and the WHO calling Diet Coke’s aspartame a possible carcinogen, Coca-Cola has branched into waters, teas, milk, alcoholic drinks in Japan and, in 2023, US cans of Jack Daniels & Coca-Cola. Pepsi dropped aspartame in 2015, restored it in 2016 and shifted toward healthier snacks and drinks.

In target audience and marketing

Pepsi has long chased the young, from “the Pepsi Generation” of the 1960s and celebrity ads to a lower price, despite its failed 2017 Kendall Jenner ad.

Coca-Cola instead sells classic Americana to older, traditional buyers, best remembered for its 1970s hilltop ad and echoed in its 2014 Super Bowl commercial.

There Are No Differences between Pepsi and Coca-Cola

Both created by pharmacists

Each began with a pharmacist. John Stith Pemberton, a Civil War veteran whose morphine habit gave way to cocaine, made French Wine Cola from coca leaves and kola nuts, then swapped the wine for sugar syrup in 1886; cocaine left the recipe in 1929.

Caleb Bradham created Pepsi in 1893 in his drug store as Brad’s Drink, an energy and digestion tonic, trademarked as Pepsi in 1898, though pepsin was never in it.

Imprinted on the brain

Research finds people barely favor either cola yet prefer both to generic brands; brain scans showed more activity in the left ventral striatum, a reward area, when people believed they had a name brand, though every sample was identical.

Surrounded by the same controversy

A Boston University study found that from 2011 to 2015 both firms funded 96 national health groups while lobbying against 28 or more bills meant to reduce soda drinking.

The Bottom Line: The two differ in taste, audience and branding but share similar origins and influence. Which do you prefer?
