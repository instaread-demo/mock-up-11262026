Little Somebody Is Bringing Hong Kong-Style Desserts to San Diego

The husband-and-wife team behind Little Someone is expanding into sweets, sharing the flavors of their childhoods at farmers markets across North County

By Beth DemmonNovember 20, 2026

The end of each year is chock-full of goodies from holiday parties, potlucks, and family meals, all including the most important element: dessert. While I’m all about the continental classics like apple pie and gingerbread cookies, it’s always a refreshing change of pace to enjoy flavors from abroad as well. So when I heard about Little Somebody, a farmers market stall serving Hong Kong-style desserts, the news hit me in all the right places (namely, my tastebuds).

Sze Nga “Sarah” Tsang and husband Siu Fung “Syrus” Kwan spun the stand off from Little Someone, their savory stall at farmers markets every day but Saturday, where Syrus, a Hong Kong culinary school graduate, has served chow mein, pan-fried dumplings, and milk teas since arriving 10 years ago. Sarah, who joined him four years ago, missed home food unlike the Cantonese-style dishes Americans call “Chinese.”

Hong Kong food is Cantonese at heart, with Western touches from 156 years of British rule: baked pastries, afternoon tea, more rice than northern China, and less spice than Sichuan.

Sarah wants diners, and even fellow vendors, to tell Asian cuisines apart.

A Thai vendor once mistook her black glutinous rice for sticky rice. They are close cousins, sweet and served with mango, but the Hong Kong and Taiwanese style is looser and darker than white Thai sticky rice, with more anthocyanins since the bran stays on; the presentation, she says, is “totally different.”

Launched in August, the dessert program now appears at markets almost daily, and the pair hope to open a shop of their own someday.

Weekly schedule for Little Somebody desserts and Little Someone savory dishes: Monday, Welks Farmers Market (Escondido); Tuesday, Escondido Farmers Market; Thursday, Oceanside (morning and evening); Friday, Rancho Bernardo; Sunday, Rancho Santa Fe.
