Low-income, Black and Latin American people less likely to have a family doctor: report

By Canadian Press on November 25, 2026.

TORONTO — A new report by the Canadian Institute for Health Information says people with low incomes and people who are Black or Latin American are less likely to have a primary-care provider.

About six million people in Canada — including both adults and children — don’t have a family doctor or nurse practitioner, leaving them “without a source of regular, ongoing care,” the report released Thursday said.

Income matters: 87 per cent of the highest-income adults have a regular provider, versus 78 per cent of the lowest-income group, the report said.

Residents of the poorest neighbourhoods were almost three times as likely to be hospitalized for issues good primary care could have handled, the institute found. Rigid low-wage jobs, child-care problems and drug costs may play a part, it suggested.

Dr. Tara Kiran, a family doctor and researcher at St. Michael’s Hospital and the University of Toronto, said the system runs on “9 to 5” hours and is hard to navigate.

For Black and Latin American adults, the rates were 66 and 68 per cent, against 86 per cent for white adults, the report said, adding that dismissed concerns and language and cultural barriers can erode trust.

Dr. Mojola Omole, who heads the Black Physicians’ Association of Ontario, said dense neighbourhoods may have too few providers, pushing people toward emergency care.

Both doctors said systemic racism feeds hesitancy. Kiran said a workforce that reflects the community helps patients feel safe, and Omole said her group aims to add more Black doctors.

The report said data on First Nations, Inuit and Métis people were not available, though CIHI acknowledges they can face greater barriers.

Provincial access ranged from 66 per cent of adults in Prince Edward Island to 89 per cent in Ontario.

The findings use CIHI hospitalization databases, Statistics Canada surveys, and provincial and territorial data.

This report by The Canadian Press was first published Nov. 26, 2026.
