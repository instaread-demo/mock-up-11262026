The warehouse behind your delivery order is unlikely to win a beauty contest. It doesn’t need to. When a useful building keeps attracting tenants and collecting rent, its lack of glamour can be an excellent feature for investors who prefer cash payments to a dazzling sales pitch.

Start with the rent

Industrial landlords collect rent from logistics and manufacturing tenants but still carry upkeep and debt.

Funds from operations (FFO) track recurring property earnings, yet FFO is not free cash, so judging Canadian REITs on yield alone falls short.

DIR

Dream Industrial REIT (TSX: DIR.UN) owns properties in Canada and Europe, adding currency and local leasing effects.

Re-leasing older contracts at higher rents lifts earnings over time; diluted FFO was $0.28 per unit in the third quarter (up 3.8%) and $1.05 for full-year 2025 (up 4.9%).

Earning income

With units at $12.87 and a $0.05979 monthly distribution, the annualized payout is $0.71748, a yield near 5.6%.

Those 777 units cost $9,999.99 and pay roughly $46.46 a month, or $557.48 a year, before trading costs.

Considerations

Units held inside a Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA) avoid Canadian tax on payouts if contribution room allows.

The issuer DRIP is suspended, and refinancing costs, vacancies and weaker property values mean the monthly payout is not guaranteed.

Bottom line

The article calls Dream Industrial a tangible income starting point within a diversified portfolio.
