800 football pitches' worth of new woodland planned for South Downs

25th November South Downs National Park Woodland Environment
By Charlie Harrison Live Reporter

A major new woodland could soon take root as part of an ambitious climate project.

The South Downs National Park Authority has announced plans to create 500 hectares of woodland across Hampshire and Sussex - roughly the size of 800 football pitches.

The new woodland is expected to capture 100,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide over the next 25 years.

Nick Heasman of the park authority called trees "vital ecological powerhouses" and said national parks have a key role against climate change, drought, flooding and pollution.

Launched in National Tree Week, it invites landowners to offer planting sites and should offset as much carbon as 54,000 petrol cars emit each year.

Legal & General backs the scheme, which sits beside Trees for the Downs, a South Downs Trust drive planting almost 15,000 trees at 16 sites this winter.
