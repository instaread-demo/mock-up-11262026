A little more of the old Death Valley? Clemson heard its fans ahead of rivalry Saturday

Grayson Mann ·Staff Writer ·16 hours ago

CLEMSON - Clemson fans have spoken up about changes to the gameday environment.

In an interview conducted earlier in November, Athletic Director Graham Neff said that changes are coming with a more traditional format.

We have learned what's coming.

TigerNet had the chance to speak with Jeff Kallin, the Senior Associate Athletic Director for Communications, about what Clemson fans should expect to see for the home finale.

Kallin said the department wants a true read on its fan base, noting how much the seven home Saturdays in Memorial Stadium mean, especially during Thanksgiving week and the South Carolina rivalry.

Fans shared feedback on the atmosphere after the Georgia Tech home game, and everyone from the gameday crew up to Neff has taken it seriously. Kallin said supporters should notice a difference this weekend.

The first sign is already on the field: orange end zones, one of them reading "IPTAY," which have already drawn buzz on social media.

Some timeout presentations will be trimmed or moved to pregame, and the PA announcer will have fewer reads, leaving more room for Tiger Band, the cheerleaders and the Rally Cats. Blake Whiten will also perform at Fan Fest at The Stacks.

The game day script now features all four traditional cheers, Spellaround (fans spelling C-L-E-M-S-O-N section by section), Cadence Count, Orange-White and Clemson Tigers, plus a refreshed playlist.

Kallin said the staff sorted out what belongs in-game, what could shift to pregame and what could go, all to keep the energy high, with hopes that more than 81,000 fans make the Palmetto Bowl one to remember.

Saturday will also inform plans for the 2027 campaign, as some new traditions may stick and others fade. Kallin said the department will keep listening and is eager to welcome 81,500 for the 3 p.m. kickoff.
