SEATTLE (AP) — The United States’ hopes for a deep World Cup run at home ended when Charles De Ketelaere scored twice and assisted on another goal, helping Belgium expose the Americans’ defensive liabilities in a 4-1 win Monday night that earned a quarterfinal berth.

While the U.S. was boosted by the presence of star forward Folarin Balogun, whose one-game red-card suspension was controversially lifted by FIFA, American defenders were at fault in a pair of first-half goals and goalkeeper Matt Freese's gaffe gave the Red Devils a third early in the second half.

Romelu Lukaku, a second-half substitute, closed the scoring in stoppage time after a Chris Richards turnover.

Coach Mauricio Pochettino, who kicked over a rack of water bottles after Belgium's second goal, said his side “didn’t connect with the game.”

Belgium, unbeaten in 18 and seeking a first title, has now ousted the U.S. in the round of 16 twice in 12 years. It faces Spain on Friday in Inglewood for a semifinal spot against France or Morocco.

Malik Tillman's 31st-minute free kick made it 1-1, but Belgium went back ahead 61 seconds later. Christian Pulisic hurt his right foot on a 52nd-minute shot and came off soon after.

The Americans missed out on a first quarterfinal since 2002, lost to Belgium for a seventh straight time and have dropped 11 of 12 against European teams.

De Ketelaere opened the scoring in the eighth minute and later set up Hans Vanaken's 57th-minute goal after a Freese error, in front of 66,925 at Lumen Field. All six CONCACAF teams are now out.

Belgium, with Jérémy Doku and Kevin DeBruyne on the bench, pressed from the start; De Ketelaere's 33rd-minute header was his eighth international goal.

Lukaku, on in the 67th minute, scored his 93rd international goal. Vanaken had come on for Amadou Onana, whose knee injury coach Rudi Garcia feared was serious.
