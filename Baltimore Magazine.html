Baltimore Colleges That Donate Their Leftover Dining Hall Food Make a Massive Impact

Student volunteers fight back against food insecurity in their communities, especially around the holidays.

By Gabrielle Plaag | November 23, 2026, 12:11 pm

Mondays and Thursdays at Loyola University’s new York Road Community Center are all about giving back. In a sunlit room, around 10-15 student volunteers gather to package grilled chicken, salmon, rice, and beans in to-go boxes headed for the local community fridge. They chat and sing upbeat tunes as they work together with one goal—to end hunger in Baltimore’s families.

As the holidays near, the Baltimore Office of Sustainability counts 22 percent of residents, or 138,150 people, as food insecure, and SNAP cuts have added strain. Since about a quarter of city trash is food, big campuses are donating their surplus meals.

The Food Recovery Network (FRN), founded by students at the University of Maryland, College Park in 2011, now has hundreds of chapters.

Baltimore’s five chapters are at Baltimore City Community College, Goucher College, Loyola University, Stevenson University, and Towson University; Loyola’s has saved almost 5,000 pounds since September 2025.

Twice weekly, its volunteers turn about 150 pounds of food into meals for the 5104 York Road fridge, one of 38 in the Bmore Community Fridge Network, which FRN joined last year.

Traci James, moderator of Loyola’s FRN, says 10 to 42 trays go unused weekly at the largest dining hall, yet the fridge empties within an hour.

Noting the York Road corridor has no grocery stores, she calls the citywide problem food apartheid, rooted in redlining.

A Protestant minister, James sees cutting waste as part of her calling. She welcomes donations and volunteers, and Loyola is building an app listing leftover event meals for students.

Its motto urges taking what you need and sharing what you can.

How to Help
Local ways to pitch in this season:

  • Food Matters Program: Sunday food-scrap drop-off, 7 a.m. to 12 p.m., Baltimore Farmers Market under the JFX.
  • A city list of food scrap haulers.
  • 4MyCity Inc. (founder Christopher Dipnarine): 300-350 families a day, 44 million pounds in 2025, donations welcome.
